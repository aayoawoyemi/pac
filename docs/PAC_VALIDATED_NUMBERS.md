# PAC: validated numbers (2026-09-29)

**Thesis (possession scarcity):** possessions are scarce (when a player sits, his team re-takes 99% of
his shots), so every possession a player uses has a price: what his team would have gotten from it in
someone else's hands. Every efficiency stat assumes that price is league average. We measure it, and it
depends on how many of the team's possessions he takes. PAC is the stat that charges the measured price.

Every number here is regenerated by a script (listed at the bottom). Supersedes the coefficient
numbers in the earlier coefficient record (`docs/COEFFICIENT_RECORD.md`). Units: points per shooting
possession (TSA = FGA + 0.44 FTA); ×50 = true-shooting points; ×100 = points per 100 attempts.
`L` = the absent player's season share of on-court possessions (TSA/POSS), the variable PAC prices on.
Brackets = 95% intervals, clustered by team-season (the cluster bootstrap agrees).

## The design (one primary design, replacing the four old ones)

Team-game regression, 1997-98 to 2025-26. The outcome is the team's points per shooting possession
over the whole game. Every absence of every rotation player is modeled at once:

    team efficiency = fixed effects + controls + Σ(absent) L_full × [(league price − his efficiency) − gap(L)]

- the first term is accounting: his own points and attempts leave with him
- `gap(L)` is the replacement price, and it is estimated
- fixed effects: roster spell (the team-season is split at every core player's arrival, departure
  or season-ending injury), opponent-season, season × month
- controls: home, own and opponent rest (back-to-back, 2 days, 3+ days), opponent absences

Sample:
- 68,708 team-games (34,354 games)
- 10,510 rotation player-team-seasons, 108,895 absences
- 863 team-season clusters
- absent players' share: median 0.184, 5th–95th percentile 0.123–0.277
- players at 24%+ share: 15,885 absences

## Mechanism (new, and the most interesting finding)

Split team efficiency into "who takes the shots" (each shooter at his own usual efficiency) and
"how well they shoot" (actual minus usual):

| component | loading on his share (s) | loading on his own efficiency edge |
|---|---|---|
| who shoots (mix) | **−0.009 [−0.045, 0.026]** | 0.836 [0.811, 0.862] |
| how well they shoot (rate) | **0.635 [0.487, 0.783]** | −0.054 [−0.142, 0.033] |

**The load premium comes entirely from the remaining players shooting worse, not from worse
shooters taking the shots.** The "rate" part includes both the absorbers' own usage-efficiency
tradeoff and lost creation or spacing; this design cannot separate those two.

The accounting coefficient on his own efficiency edge is 0.782 [0.695, 0.869] (identity says 1).
By share:
- 0.61 [0.45, 0.77] below 18%
- 0.83 below 24%
- **0.88 [0.71, 1.04] at 24%+**

So for high-usage players the team loses his full efficiency edge. For low-usage role players,
like-for-like backups recover about 40% of it. The price schedule is unchanged whichever way this
term is estimated.

## Retire from the old record

- "four partially non-overlapping designs": replaced by one design plus the validation battery above
- in-game rest 0.324: contaminated by bench-unit lineups; drop

## Is PAC better than TS Add / UW-rTS? (UW-rTS = TS Add / 100)

What teams actually lose per game when a player sits (team points, same fixed effects and controls)
vs what each stat says he is worth (`scripts/12_pac_vs_tsadd.py`):

| absent player's share | his PPG | actually lost | TS Add says | PAC says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| under 15% | 5.9 | −0.39 [−0.59, −0.19] | −0.08 | −0.06 |
| 15–20% | 7.6 | −0.12 [−0.28, 0.03] | −0.21 | −0.04 |
| 20–25% | 11.1 | 0.32 [0.13, 0.51] | −0.25 | 0.27 |
| 25–30% | 17.7 | **2.00 [1.68, 2.32]** | **0.07** | **1.27** |
| 30%+ | 25.1 | **3.52 [2.92, 4.12]** | **0.52** | **2.80** |

- Error at 30%+ share: TS Add −3.0 points a game, PAC −0.7 (published price −1.2). Mean absolute error
  over all absences: TS Add 0.50, PAC 0.19.
- PAC still misses ~20% at the top (unpriced creation). Part of the fit is built in (the validated price
  was estimated on absences); the published price, from the older design, shows the same pattern.
- Year-over-year stability: PPG 0.86, PAC 0.77, TS Add 0.68.

## Predicting games (`archive/scripts/_pac_predict_games.py`, hinge price)

34,354 games, cross-fitted team offense/defense ratings, missing players subtracted at their per-game value:

| a missing player costs his team... | fitted scale k (1 = the number is the true cost) | winner accuracy | accuracy when one side is missing much more |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing | — | 66.8% | 63.0% |
| his points | **0.06** | 67.1% | 64.9% |
| his vPTS / PTS+ | 0.06 | 67.2% | 65.1–65.2% |
| his TS Add | 0.75 | 67.1% | 65.7% |
| **his PAC** | **0.87** | **67.4%** | **66.5%** |

When a player sits his team loses about 6% of his points; PAC per game is close to the true cost.
Team-season level: team PAC vs win% 0.73 (TS Add 0.71, PPG 0.54); net PAC 0.89 (margin ceiling 0.97).
Team load credit is nearly constant (≈4 points a game, ±0.5), so PAC's value is in splitting credit and
pricing absences, not in team-season win prediction.

## vPTS and PTS+

Both rank players 0.991 like points (vPTS moves 0.7 points a game on average; 9 of the top 10 unchanged).
vPTS sums to the team's real score; PTS+ adds ~4.3 points per team-game that never happened. Keep points
as the description and PAC as the verdict; vPTS is optional box-score packaging; drop PTS+.

## Per-team prices: tested, not supported (`scripts/16_team_price.py`)

- one team-season's price has SE ±0.126 points per attempt, twice the effect itself
- a team's first-half price predicts its second half at r = 0.03; team prices predict worse out of sample
- franchises: true spread 0.000; same franchise year to year r = 0.05
- another 25%+ creator playing that night: gap shift −0.003 [−0.013, +0.008]; extra 20%+ teammates: −0.003

The price is structural, not organizational. This answers the deep-bench / thin-bench objection with a
test. Power: franchise differences above ~±0.045 points per attempt (2 TS points) would have been seen.

## How close to the true price? (`scripts/15_known_answer.py`)

Known-answer test: real games, fixed effects, absences and noise; estimated effect removed, a known price
planted, residuals reshuffled within team-season, 40 replicates each:

| planted | true gap at 30% | recovered threshold | recovered slope | recovered gap at 30% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no effect | 0.000 | (undefined) | 0.004 (SD 0.060) | 0.001 (SD 0.006) |
| 0.25 L, no threshold | 0.075 | 0.015 (SD 0.025) | 0.265 (SD 0.034) | 0.073 (SD 0.005) |
| 0.535 max(0, L − 0.13) | 0.091 | 0.129 (SD 0.019) | 0.537 (SD 0.075) | 0.091 (SD 0.006) |
| 0.80 max(0, L − 0.18) | 0.096 | 0.181 (SD 0.013) | 0.825 (SD 0.135) | 0.092 (SD 0.007) |

The method recovers every planted price, invents no effect when there is none, and invents no threshold when
there is none. Hidden-confounder robustness value: 5.8%. An unmeasured factor would have to be ~157x stronger
than back-to-backs on team efficiency and ~15x stronger on absences to erase the slope.

## Scarcity or lost playmaking? (`results/scarcity.md`; supersedes the earlier version of this section)

The absence cost mixes the absorbed shots (possession scarcity) with the absent player's playmaking. By the absent
player's AST% at the same share, shape left free:

| absent player | gap at 28%+ share (bins) | gap at 30% share (free line) |
|---|---|---|
| pure scorers (lowest third AST%) | 0.065 [0.020, 0.110] | 0.072 [0.043, 0.101] |
| playmakers (highest third AST%) | 0.114 | 0.106 [0.088, 0.123] |
| published PAC, 0.25 L | 0.076 | 0.075 |

At high usage, scarcity is ~60-70% of a playmaker's absence cost (the UW-rTS paper, section K, found 69/31 by a
different method), and the published 0.25 L is close to the scarcity-only price. It over-credits the 15-25% share
range, where pure scorers show no scarcity cost. An earlier version of this section (slope 0.258, 2.2 TS points)
came from a shared-threshold restriction and is retracted. See PAC_RECONCILIATION.md.

## Honest limitations

- The price is a league schedule by share, not roster-specific (deep vs thin benches).
- The replacement price includes spillovers to teammates' efficiency, by construction; the mechanism table shows that is most of it.
- Absences are not randomized. Defenses: roster-spell and opponent fixed effects, rest controls, the event study, the placebo, injury-only runs, team-month fixed effects.
- The FT 0.44 constant has not been rebuilt from play-by-play shot episodes. Weights of 0.40–0.475 move the gap at 30% share by at most 0.003.
- Gross points per shooting possession. Per possession used (including turnovers), the gap at 30% share is 0.083.
- Dates: exact from the NBA CDN feed for 2021-22 onward. For 1997–2020 they come from matching ESPN schedules in game-ID order, which was 99.9% exact where checkable in a normal season (2022-23) and 98.9% in a COVID-postponement season (2021-22). They only feed the rest controls.
